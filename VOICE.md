## Pronoun stance
Mixed — second-person ("you") when speaking directly to the nurse/student's decision, third-person for data and industry context.

## Lexical rules
- **Contractions**: allowed, keep it conversational and direct rather than corporate
- **Sentence ceiling**: 25 words max
- **Paragraph ceiling**: 100 words max (short, punchy paragraphs, not lecture-y blocks)
- **Summary label**: Key Takeaways
- **No em dashes (—)**: use commas or regular dashes instead
- **No bold text** in body copy

## Headline patterns
- **Favor**: direct, benefit-or-decision headlines that speak to the nurse's actual choice (e.g. "Study Nursing Abroad: What It Actually Pays Off in 2026")
- **Avoid**: fear-based or guilt-based framing, generic "Ultimate Guide" clickbait, corporate-speak

## Voice fingerprint
- Funny vs serious: 0.2 (mostly serious, occasional warmth)
- Formal vs casual: 0.4 (conversational-professional, not stiff)
- Respectful vs irreverent: 0.85 (respectful, peer-to-peer, not condescending)
- Enthusiastic vs matter-of-fact: 0.6 (aspirational but grounded in real numbers, not hype)

## Readability target
- Audience tier: consumer (nursing students/professionals, and parents), intelligent general reader
- Flesch Grade: 7-9
- Flesch Ease: 60-70 (accessible, conversational, not academic)

## Brand voice notes (from Jusst Nursing brand-reference.md)
- Personality: warm, reassuring, professional, a knowledgeable senior nurse or mentor guiding a junior colleague
- Vibe: aspirational but grounded, real numbers, real timelines, real outcomes
- Core message every piece should reinforce: nursing abroad is a structured, achievable career upgrade, guided end-to-end rather than handled piecemeal across separate vendors
